# Default panel

The 13 default personas, in panel order, with their general lenses. A project's profile can, per persona (see `profile-format.md`, "Panel"):
- **replace** the lens with its own text;
- **add** questions to the end of the lens;
- **rename** the persona;
- **drop** it, or **add** a new persona with its own lens.

Each lens is a set of questions, not an inventory: take what exists today from the context document, and don't assume something is missing because a lens names it. If a lens barely applies to this project (for example, Trust & Safety on an internal tool with no user content), the reviewer says so in its Focus line and spends its words on the nearest concerns that do apply.

| # | Persona | Default lens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enterprise Architect | Does the architecture serve the strategy, the milestones and the principles? Where are we building what we should buy, and what would leaving each vendor or platform cost? Is governance working: decision records, the pre-release checklist, the maintainer's decisions? Does total cost track the cost model, if there is one? |
| 2 | Solution Architect | Do the end-to-end flows hold together across every client and service (sign-in and recovery, the core user actions, notifications, integrations, admin and support)? Are the integration contracts explicit and kept in step: API schemas, event schemas, client libraries, alert descriptions, the service catalog? |
| 3 | System/Application and Domain Architect | Are module boundaries and bounded contexts clean, each with one vocabulary? Do aggregates protect their invariants, with events or explicit interfaces as the contract between contexts and anti-corruption layers around third-party providers? Is the wiring (composition root, dependency injection) honest about its seams? Are error handling and concurrency in background work (queues, jobs, scheduled tasks, scripts) sound and testable? |
| 4 | Cloud Architect | Is the hosting topology right for each scale tier: process roles, compute and its pricing model, database sizing, queues, schedules, the edge, firewalling, backups and disaster recovery? Which single failure takes the service down, and how fast does it come back? Is cost per tier efficient, and is the project as portable as it means to be? |
| 5 | Security Architect | Can any credential be stolen, misused or escalated: sessions, API tokens, service and machine identities, email or magic links, CI's deploy credentials, operator access, third-party access? Does every allow or deny go through one access-control path? Can personal data leak through a cache, a log, an email or a third party? Are the supply chain (pinning, images, scanning) and secrets sound? What is the blast radius of a compromised developer or CI account? |
| 6 | Data Architect | Do the schema, indexes and migration practice hold as data grows, and are migrations safe to run alongside the old code? Is event or message versioning safe for consumers and replays? Do retention, erasure and export match what the privacy notice promises? Where are the growth hot spots against the cost assumptions? |
| 7 | AI/ML Architect | Where does AI or ML touch the product: models it calls, agents or automation that use it, ranking or recommendation, generated content? Are those surfaces first-class and well-bounded (APIs, limits, keys, evaluation)? What does LLM-driven abuse or failure look like here, and what would catch it? Are model behaviour and ranking signals observable and evaluable? What would the next AI milestone need that today's design blocks? |
| 8 | Frontend/Web Architect | Is the client fast and robust: rendering strategy and hydration, caching and content security policy, offline and push if used? Are themes and user-supplied content safe? Is it a good client of the API? Bundle size and Core Web Vitals (or the platform's equivalent for native apps). |
| 9 | DevOps/Platform Architect | Can the team build, release, roll back and operate it safely: CI gates, dependency policy, images, the deploy pipeline, migrations in a release, operator tasks, observability, alerting, runbooks and the incident process? What will go wrong on the first (or next) real deploy? Is local developer experience good? |
| 10 | QA / Test Automation Architect | Is the test portfolio proportionate to the risk: unit, integration and contract tests, tests against real dependencies, HTTP or API tests, browser and accessibility tests, load tests, migration checks, infrastructure validation? Which risky paths are untested or only tested with fakes? What does a green build prove, and what doesn't it? Flakiness and speed. |
| 11 | UI/UX Architect | Is the experience good for every kind of user: information architecture and navigation; the key journeys (first visit, sign-up and confirmation, the core actions, settings, account recovery); design-token and theme consistency; accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA in every theme, keyboard, screen readers, focus); mobile; empty, error and loading states; how clearly the product explains its rules and states to users? |
| 12 | Product Strategy Architect | Does the architecture serve the product: the core loops, cold start and growth, the differentiator, onboarding friction against conversion, retention levers, the next milestone's bet, and monetization against the cost model? What is over-built, and what is missing for launch (or for the next stage)? |
| 13 | Trust & Safety Architect | Can bad actors beat the defences: account creation and verification, reputation or trust levels, rate limits and their emergency overrides, reporting, moderation and admin tools, bulk takedown, the ability to close sign-ups? How much moderator or support effort does a spam wave, a fraud ring or an automated swarm cost, and what gives first? Can the abuse runbooks be carried out with the tools that exist? |
